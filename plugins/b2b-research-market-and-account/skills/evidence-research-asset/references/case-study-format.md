# Case study format

A rewrite of a general case study template for this skill. Nothing in a case study is invented: every metric comes from the customer with its baseline and period, and every quote is written or approved by the person quoted.

## Before drafting (ask, do not invent)
- Permission: may the customer be named? Who at the customer approves the text and the quotes?
- The customer's industry and size band, in the customer's own description.
- The problem in the customer's words, ideally from an interview or written notes.
- What was delivered and when; who was involved (roles, not names, unless the people agree).
- Results: each metric with its baseline, its period, how it was measured, and who measured it. A result with no baseline is described in words, not as a multiplier.
- Anything the customer does not want public.

## Structure
1. The customer: industry and size band, in their words; what they do. [User to add: approved description]
2. The challenge: the specific problem and what it cost them, as they describe it; what they tried before; how they chose.
3. What was done: what was delivered, the timeline, the roles involved.
4. Results: metrics with baseline, period and method, each approved by the customer. [Customer to confirm: metric, baseline, period]
5. In their words: quotes written or approved by the person quoted. [Quote to be written or approved by the person quoted]
6. What others can take from it: lessons the customer agrees with; who else the approach fits, without promising the same results.

## Rules
- Placeholders stay visible until filled: "[User to add: ...]", "[Customer to confirm: ...]".
- Never write a sample quote that could be mistaken for a real one. Offer interview questions instead.
- Never upgrade a status: a pilot is a pilot, a prospect is not a customer, "worked on" is not "built".
- Every metric goes on the number lock sheet, and the case study, its social posts and the sales deck use the same figures.
- Before publishing, the customer approves the final text, and the approval is recorded by the user.

## Interview questions to offer when quotes are missing
- What was happening before you started that made this a priority?
- What did you try first, and why did it not work?
- What changed in your team's week once it was in place?
- How do you measure the result, and what was the number before?
- What would you tell a peer considering the same move?
