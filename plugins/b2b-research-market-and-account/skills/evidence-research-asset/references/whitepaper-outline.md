# White paper outline

A rewrite of a general white paper template for this skill: the structure is kept; every number, case study and outcome is sourced or asked for. Lengths are the user's to set; the ones below are starting points the user can change.

## Inputs to ask for (ask, do not invent)
- Topic and the one argument the paper makes (the thesis), in the user's words.
- The reader: role, decision they are making, what they already know. Name the job the reader hires the paper for (for example "defend a budget conversation", not "learn the topic").
- Length and format, and where it will be published.
- Sources: the research the user owns or cites, and the source boundary (attached only, or web allowed).
- Case studies: which customers, with written permission to be named, and who approves their quotes.
- Brand guidelines, if any.

## Structure
1. Executive summary (about one page): the problem in the reader's terms; three to five findings, each with its source; the recommendation; what it changes for the reader. Every figure from the number lock sheet.
2. Introduction (about half a page): context, the research method (what was read, counted or surveyed, the base, the period), and what the paper does not cover.
3. Problem analysis (two to three pages): the current state with sourced evidence; the pain points named by real sources (customers who agreed to be cited, published surveys with their base); the cost of doing nothing only if a source quantifies it, otherwise described in words without a number.
4. Approach (two to three pages): the proposed way to solve it; what implementation involves; examples only from real, permitted cases ("[User to add: case, with permission]"); expected outcomes stated as what to measure, not as promised numbers.
5. Recommendations (one to two pages): next steps, a timeline the user sets, the measures of success the user will track, and the risks with how to reduce them.
6. Conclusion (about half a page): what the reader should take away and the one action to take.
7. Appendices: the data tables, the full method, and every reference with its link and date.

## Rules
- No statistic without a source; no "studies show"; no multipliers with no source.
- Each statistic is checked for what it measured (region, sample, buyer type, consumer or business, date) before it supports a claim (see whitepaper-review.md, lens 1).
- Charts show locked numbers only, with the source under each chart.
- A case study without permission is written as an anonymised situation only if the user describes it, and labelled so.
