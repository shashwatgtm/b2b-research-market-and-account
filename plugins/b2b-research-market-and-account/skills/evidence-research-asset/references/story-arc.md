# Story before numbers: myth, truth, dominance, twist, verdict

A research asset is read for its story; the numbers prove it. Choose the story first, then test it against the locked numbers. If the data does not support the story, change the story.

## The five beats
| Beat | What it does | What it needs from the data |
|---|---|---|
| Myth | States what the reader commonly believes, in the reader's words | a belief the user can name (from their customers, sales calls or public commentary they can point to); never an invented "most people think" |
| Truth | Shows what the data says instead | one locked number, with its base and period |
| Dominance | Shows where the effect is largest or most concentrated | a breakdown from the same source (a sector, a region, a type), same base |
| Twist | Shows the finding that cuts against the obvious reading | a second locked number that complicates the first (a small share with a large impact, a rise in one place and a fall in another) |
| Verdict | Says what the reader should check or decide, without overclaiming | follows only from the beats above; no new number |

## Rules
- One theme per page or post. A series splits the beats across posts; each post still stands on its own with its source.
- The headline never says more than the data. If the data shows a share of detections, the headline does not say "risk", "losses" or "attacks" unless the source does.
- No catastrophe openers and no fear selling. Inform and create curiosity; do not shout.
- The beats are tested before drafting: write each beat as one line with its lock sheet id. A beat with no id is cut or turned into a question; a beat that rests on words, not a number, carries its short exact quote, where it was found and the date instead (topline-and-number-lock.md, section 2b), or it is cut or labelled as the writer's view.
- If the user's preferred story is not in the data, say so plainly and offer the story the data supports.
- A benchmark enters the story only next to the finding it is compared with, like for like (topline-and-number-lock.md, section 2).
- Modify the best existing version rather than rebuilding from scratch when the user is iterating.

## Example beat sheet (shape only; the ids point to a lock sheet)
- Myth: [the belief the user names, with where they heard it]
- Truth: L1
- Dominance: L3
- Twist: L4 with L1
- Verdict: [the check or decision for the reader, no new number]
