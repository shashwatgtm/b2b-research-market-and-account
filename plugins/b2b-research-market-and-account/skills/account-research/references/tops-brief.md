# The one account brief, in TOPS order

TOPS is the order the brief is built and read in: Technographics, Organisation, Person, Situation. Every finding has a source and a status, and every finding becomes a probe question for the meeting. The person block covers only the person's own public professional voice, and every personality note in it is labelled as an inference, never as a fact.

## Header
- Account: legal name as on its filing or site, trade name if different, website.
- Prepared for: the user's meeting (date, role being met, the user's goal for the meeting, as the user said), and whether the account already buys from the user (from the user's CRM or connector, or the account's own pages). If it does, the brief opens with what it already uses, each item sourced; a competitor product is named only from the account's own pages or filings, a case study the vendor and the account published together, or the user's records (one that rests only on an enrichment row is not named: the field reads "a product reported by [tool], not named until the account's own pages or your records show it", status unverified).
- Sources used, with fetch dates; sources the user banned; the user's connector, if one was used.
- Coverage note: which blocks are complete, which are partial.

## T: Technographics
What the account runs, shown by evidence the account itself published.
- Fields (the user may narrow them, for example "only ERP, cloud and tax suite"): core platforms (ERP, CRM), cloud provider, data and analytics stack, security tools, integrations it offers or consumes, developer platforms.
- Acceptable evidence: the account's own product, integration, partner, careers and engineering pages; its filings; case studies published by the vendor and the account together; the user's enrichment connector, as a lead until confirmed (next line).
- Not evidence: a guess from the industry, a vendor's logo wall without the account confirming, a job post older than the user's cutoff without saying so. A technographic or enrichment database row is a lead only: it stays unverified until the account's own pages, a filing, a joint case study or a second independent source confirms it.
- When web tools are available, open the account's careers and engineering pages (job posts name the tools a team uses, and each is dated) before writing not found.
- When nothing is found: "not found on the pages I opened: [the pages]" (naming any search run) and a probe question that asks; a few pages never prove that nothing is public.

## O: Organisation
How the account is set up and where it is going, from its own disclosures. When the team or segment the user asked about does not exist at the account, or sits under another name, say so in one line with the source; never map it silently to the user's term.
- Fields: legal entity and listing status, headquarters, regions served, business units and products, size band (employees or revenue only from a filing or the account's own page), recent results (with period and label exactly as filed), leadership roles relevant to the user's offer (titles only), recent organisation changes from the account's releases.
- Revenue and profit: copy the label as filed ("Revenue", "Revenue from operations", "EBITDA", "PAT") with the period; never mix labels, and never gloss a label from memory (EBITDA is written as EBITDA, not explained as another measure); a definition appears only when the filing or the account's own pages give it, quoted with its source.

## P: Person (the role, then the person's public professional voice)
- Who: the people the user will meet or must reach, named by the user, or the holder of a role the user named (from the account's own leadership page, releases or filings first; otherwise from a dated trade report checked against a second source, with the block headed "Depends on [name] holding this role: reported by [source, date]; not yet confirmed on the account's own pages", and "reported, not yet confirmed" beside the name wherever else the brief uses it). A person the user named keeps the block when a source gives them another title or names someone else in the role: each title is shown with its source and date, and the other holder is not researched unless the user asks. When sources name different holders, the account's own page wins; otherwise ask which person the user will meet. Do not research people the user did not ask for by name or role. When the role the user names does not exist at the account or sits under another title, say so in one line with the source; never map it silently, and research the holder of the other title only if the user asks.
- Purpose limit: the person block is for preparing this meeting only. Say so in the brief; do not offer it as a profile to keep, merge into a CRM record or share beyond the people preparing the meeting.
- Allowed sources, and only these: the person's own public professional statements: their own public posts on professional topics, published interviews (trade press included), conference talks, podcasts they spoke on, quotes in the account's releases and results calls. Every item carries its link and date, and the page ties it to the same person (their name with this account, or with an employer a cited source gives). Use a public profile, post or handle for a person only when the account's own page links to it or the user confirms it is theirs; people share names. Never build or guess a handle.
- What the block holds, in this order:
  - Quotes: their words, verbatim, with link and date.
  - Themes: the priorities and problems they return to across statements, each with the statements it rests on.
  - Likes and dislikes, only as they stated them in public ("what I look for in a vendor is ...", "I do not like ..."), quoted with link and date.
  - Personality notes: how they come across in those statements (for example direct, data led, story led, sceptical of hype). Each note is written as "Inference from [statement, link, date]: ...", never as a fact, and needs at least one cited statement; a note with no statement behind it is not written.
- Never collected, inferred or written, even if asked: health, religion, politics, family, private life, ethnicity or caste, sexual orientation, home address or area, personal phone numbers, personal email addresses or any other personal contact details; anything from private, closed or leaked sources (private groups, leaked documents, data broker files, scraped personal data); anything read from likes, follows, reactions, connections or posting times rather than the person's own words. If the user asks for any of these, decline in one line and offer the allowed block instead.
- When nothing is found: "no public professional statement found on the pages I opened: [the pages]" (naming any search run) and a probe question that asks in the meeting; never guess a priority, a like or a trait.
- In shared examples, people are written as roles ("the head of IT"); nothing is claimed about the real person holding a role.

## S: Situation
What is happening at the account now that makes a conversation timely.
- Fields: results and guidance (as filed), launches, expansion into new regions, funding or listing events, partnerships, regulatory changes that apply to it, hiring patterns visible on its own careers page, and posts on its official company pages (each dated and linked, never scraped against a platform's terms).
- The signal test: a real signal is visible from outside, it is recent, and it points to a job the buyer needs done now. Miss one of the three and it is still a demographic, not a signal. Label each item "signal" or "context".

## Probe questions
- One per finding row, context rows included (a person block's like, dislike or personality note row may point to the probe of the statement it rests on), written so the account can answer from its own experience, and tied to the finding it comes from: "Your site lists 290+ integrations. How does a new connector get reviewed before it goes live?"
- Neutral, no leading claims about the account's problems, no fear selling.
- When the account already buys from the user, the first probe questions are about the next need, never about what it already bought.
- Top questions: three to five for a first meeting unless the user asks for more; the strongest first (tied to a signal, not to context).

## Call plan (when asked)
When the user asks to be prepared for a call (for example "prep me for the call" or "a call plan"; a request for a brief alone gets the brief and its top questions), add a call plan after the probe questions: the questions for the opening minutes (the strongest probe questions, signals first); under each, what to listen for (the answer that would confirm or weaken the finding behind it); what to avoid (any claim on the unverified list, any assumption about the account's problems); and one qualifying question built from the user's own must haves or disqualifiers on the ICP card, or asked of the user if there is no card. Length is the user's to set.

## ABM pilot ideas (only when asked)
- Each idea is tied to a finding and a probe question, and names the user's channel or team that would run it.
- No budget, uplift, conversion rate or benchmark unless the user supplies it.
- Ask for the user's capacity and channel limits before ranking ideas.

## Format of a finding
| Block | Finding | Source (link, page) | Date | Status | Probe question |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Situation | Results for the quarter ended 30 June 2026: "Revenue grew by 14.3% YoY to ₹51.4 Cr" | Unicommerce's results release on the exchange, https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/UNICOMMERCE_13082026193253_Press_Release.pdf (fetched 11 October 2026; Unicommerce is used here only as an example account) | 13 Aug 2026 | unverified (one source, the filing) | "As enterprise onboarding grows, which part of your stack takes the most review effort?" |

Person block rows use the same columns, with placeholders shown here because shared examples never describe a real person:
| Block | Finding | Source (link, page) | Date | Status | Probe question |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Person: quote | "[their words, verbatim]" | [link to the interview, talk, post or release] | [date] | as published | "[a question that lets them expand on it]" |
| Person: stated like or dislike | "[what they said they look for or dislike, verbatim]" | [link] | [date] | as published | "[a question that lets them say more about it]" |
| Person: personality note | Inference from [statement, link, date]: [for example, comes across as data led] | the statement it rests on | [date] | inference, not a fact | see the probe on [the statement it rests on] |

Status values: verified, unverified, not found (see source-and-verification.md). Person block rows use "as published" only when the page holding the statement was opened in this session (a statement the user pasted is "as pasted, not yet checked"), and "inference, not a fact" for every personality note.
