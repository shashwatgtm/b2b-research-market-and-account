# Sources and claim strength

## Order of sources
1. Sources the user named. They beat the skill's own search. If the user says "check against the filing only", other sources are listed as differing, not used.
2. Primary: the subject's filings and results releases, the publisher's own report or topline tables, regulators, official statistics, the product's own documentation for product facts.
3. The subject's own website and releases: written as the subject's statement ("the company says").
4. Reputed publications that name their source.
5. Never: a link not opened in this session, search snippets as the source of a claim (when a page cannot be opened, a snippet from a search for its wording may be shown beside the claim, labelled "from a search result; the page itself was not opened", and the claim stays unverified), pages that name no source for their figures, pages that say their content may be machine generated without a named source, anonymous "research shows", sources the user banned.

## When the named source is unavailable
Say so plainly ("the About page did not render the user count in the fetch"), leave the cell blank, and ask whether another source may be used. Never fill the cell from memory or from a different source without the user's word.

## A claim is only as strong as its source
| Source says | Do not write | Write |
|---|---|---|
| 29.5 percent | "1 in 3" | "about 3 in 10" or "29.5 percent" |
| 2 of 3 companies tested | "companies" or "all" | "2 of the 3 companies tested" |
| The company calls itself "India's #1" | "India's #1" | "which describes itself as India's #1 ..." |
| A figure from 2023 | "has" (present tense) | "said in September 2023 that it had ..." |
| A share of a subset | "of all ..." | "of [the subset the source names]" |
| A survey of consumers | a claim about business buyers | nothing, unless a business source exists |
| Detections in a vendor's telemetry | "attacks in India" | "detections recorded by [the publisher]" |
| One example | "customers see" | "one customer reported" |
| Nothing confirms the count | a range, "about" or "+" | the count from the record, or the placeholder |
| Nothing on the motive | "X did this to ..." | what X said or did, with its source, and the writer's view labelled as a view, without a motive for X |

## Recency
The recency cutoff is set per job by the user (for example "nothing older than a set month" or "this region only"). Ask when it matters and is not given. When a data point fails the cutoff or the region, find a replacement that fits, with its link, or say none was found on the pages opened and the searches run (name them).

## Like for like
Comparisons hold only between the same product type, region, period and measure. A comparison without a matched source is unverified. Negative comparisons in examples never name a real company.

## Counts, dates and names
- Counts are checked even when the user is sure of them. The user's first hand facts about their own work stand, but a count is still checked against the record or a source.
- Dates: the weekday next to a date must match the calendar; a document meant to be current must not carry last year's date.
- Names and titles are copied exactly from the source; never upgraded (prospect is not partner, pilot is not customer, co-founder is not founder, beta is not generally available, supported is not led, relaunched is not launched).
