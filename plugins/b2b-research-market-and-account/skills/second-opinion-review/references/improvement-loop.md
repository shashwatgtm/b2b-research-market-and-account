# Questions first, then options, then one final version

A rewrite of a general recursive improvement template for this skill. The useful part kept: ask before judging, offer a few directions when the purpose allows several, then converge. Dropped: fixed counts, demographic questions, and steering toward a stronger call to action by default.

## Step 1. Ask the questions that change the verdicts
Ask only what is missing and would change a verdict, in one message, at most three:
- Who reads this, and what do they already know?
- What should the reader think or do after reading?
- How will you know it worked (replies, saves, a meeting booked, a decision taken)?
Also confirm, if not given: the channel and its rules, any lines that must not change, and the version to work on.
Do not ask what the conversation, the files or the draft itself already answer. If the user says "just do it", proceed with the purpose as best read from the draft and state that reading in one line.

## Step 2. Options, only when the purpose allows several
When the draft missed its purpose, or the purpose can be served in more than one way, offer two or three directions:
- each with its opening line or angle;
- one line on why it fits the reader and the objective;
- one line on what it gives up.
Recommend one, with the reason. If the purpose is clear and the draft serves it, skip this step and say so.

## Step 3. One final version
After the user picks (or accepts the recommendation, or asked for the final text directly), write one complete final version that applies every accepted, edited and modified point, and none of the denied ones. Then, at most one line offering a further change the user may want (shorter, more formal), never a list of menus.

## Rushed users
If the user asks for the final text straight away, give the final version first, then the verdict table in short form, then any question that is still open.
